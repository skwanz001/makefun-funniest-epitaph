> I told you I was dying. You said 'same'. Fair enough.

## Why this line

The judge weighs, in order: a one-line epitaph of famous last words, a real laugh from a genuine joke, a surprise, reads carved in stone, stays in the head. The line is first-person dying speech, deadpan, and the joke turns on one word: the dying man says he told everyone he was dying, and the reply he got was "same" — the modern shrug of total indifference — and his last word is a flat "Fair enough." A lifetime of being ignored, summed up at the moment of death, accepted without a fight. No setup to explain, no stage direction, no pun: short, quotable, lands on one read.
